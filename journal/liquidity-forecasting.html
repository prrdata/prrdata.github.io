TREASURY · RISK · 5 MIN READ

A forecast is useful
when it explains the miss.

Backtesting gives a score. A Treasury team also needs to understand the movements behind it.

Cash flow and liquidity forecasting can combine transaction histories, payment calendars, seasonal patterns, deposit behavior, and market rates. A useful model selection process tests these candidates against time-ordered data rather than relying on a single holdout slice.

Respect time in validation

Walk-forward backtesting better reflects how forecasts are used: train on the past, predict the next period, move forward, and repeat. Compare error measures such as MAPE and RMSE, while checking that the chosen metric matches the business decision and the scale of the series.

Explain variance in operational terms

When an actual balance differs from forecast, decompose the likely drivers: a large payment, calendar effects, changed deposit behavior, or a rate movement. Pair that view with scenario and stress analysis so users can see how liquidity buffers behave under different conditions.

Ground explanations

An LLM layer can translate model drivers into plain language, but it should retrieve relevant policy and limit documents, cite the supporting passages, and clearly distinguish observed facts from generated interpretation. Keep the forecast calculation separate from the narrative layer.

Forecast quality improves when model monitoring and user feedback are treated as part of the same loop. A miss is a signal to investigate and learn, not just a number to report.
