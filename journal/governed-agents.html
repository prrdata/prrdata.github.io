Agentic AI needs
a decision trail.

In a regulated workflow, a model answer is one step in a case, not the end of it.

Agentic workflows can coordinate document extraction, verification, policy lookup, and risk scoring. That makes them useful in areas like KYC, where work is naturally split into stages. It also means the system needs to explain what happened at every stage.

Make each step inspectable

A practical design gives every agent a narrow responsibility and a structured output. Record the evidence it used, the confidence of its result, and the next action it chose. Keep citations attached to retrieved policy content so a reviewer can inspect the source rather than trust a summary alone.

Every automated decision should leave enough context for a person to understand, challenge, and continue it.

Route uncertainty to a person

Human review should be part of the workflow graph. Low confidence, conflicting evidence, or a missing required document can trigger a case handoff. The reviewer should see why the case was routed and what information is still needed.

Test the whole workflow

Evaluate not only answer quality, but also routing, tool calls, retrieval grounding, and failure behavior. Keep evaluation cases that reflect edge conditions and policy changes. Monitor those signals after deployment, with versioned models and prompts.

This approach adds operational care, but it makes the system easier to improve and easier to audit. The goal is to support a qualified decision maker with evidence, not hide the decision inside a chain of model calls.
